01 — The principle

One parent brand, one city modifier

Mappa is the brand. Each edition places a city beside it, written in its local language. The core stays identical from one city to the next; only the word, the color and the language change.

Stays constant

The pictogram — the folded map (travel icon)
The word « mappa » (grotesque, lowercase)
The lockup construction & clear space
Type, paper, dot marker

Changes per city

The city name in its local language
The dedicated accent hue
The pictogram’s map fragment
Local coordinates & landmarks
02 — The logo

The travel icon

The pictogram is a travel map folded accordion-style, its dotted route and a marked spot. It is the « mappa » object itself, set before the word. Three variants: inline for the lockup, bubble for social profiles, square for app icons.

03 — Language

« Mappa », map in Interlingua

Mappa means « map » in Interlingua, an auxiliary language built from shared Romance roots. The word is intelligible to over a billion speakers without translation — Italian, Spanish, Portuguese, Romanian, and understood by ear in French and English (via map) and beyond. It is the cornerstone of a brand that travels: one word, everywhere.

04 — Declension

The Mappa family

The same gesture, four cities. Each inherits its hue and its language; the system stays legible inline, on a cover and as a pictogram.

05 — Color

One hue per city

A shared warm neutral core — aged paper, brown ink. Then one vivid accent per city, chosen for its cultural obviousness: Roman saffron, London bus red, Brussels iris green, Parisian royal blue.

07 — Best practices

Do / Don’t

● Do

✓

Inline picto + city word in its local language, in the city’s accent color.

✓

Standalone picto in bubble or square for app/profile — always in the city hue.

✕ Don’t

✕

No off-palette hues, no color that doesn’t match the city.

✕

Don’t distort, stretch, slant or split the picto from the word.
